# Case Study Humanizer

Write a clear account of someone's actual work: what prompted it, what they did, why their choices mattered, and what they can support with evidence. Humanizing is an editorial pass, not permission to invent experience.

## Establish the factual boundary

Read the user's notes, existing draft, relevant artifacts, and requested audience. Preserve exact titles, required wording, order, technical names, and the distinction between individual and team work. Use existing evidence-curation notes when available; avoid asking for facts already supplied.

Identify supported facts, author-reported observations, and unresolved claims. Keep that working record out of public prose. Ask a focused question only when a missing fact materially changes the story; continue drafting supported portions.

## Write in the author's voice

Follow supplied writing samples and the requested perspective. For a personal portfolio with no sample, use natural first-person language grounded in the author's stated role. Retain third person when the existing format requires it. Convey personality through real priorities and choices rather than invented feelings, anecdotes, quotations, or setbacks.

Build a connected narrative:

- Open with the concrete need and why the project began.
- Explain selected actions and decisions, connecting tools to their purpose. Use reasons supplied by the author; mark a proposed explanation for confirmation instead of presenting it as their motivation.
- Show the outcome at its actual stage, with an artifact, observed behavior, or measured result where available.
- Close with a supported reflection or next step. An ongoing project can end with what remains to be done.

Scale this shape to the requested length and existing layout. Prefer flowing paragraphs; add headings or lists when useful. Replace promotional abstractions with concrete actions. Use technical detail to explain decisions. Keep the result conversational and competent; artificial slang, deliberate mistakes, and detector promises add no value.

## Check meaning and deliver

Compare the rewrite with the source claim by claim. Preserve uncertainty, chronology, attribution, tool behavior, and project status. A lossless format alone does not demonstrate measured quality preservation; a working preview or pushed commit alone does not demonstrate deployment or impact. Omit unsupported metrics and superlatives, or qualify observations when they matter to the story.

Keep private media and restricted material private. Explain the workflow through approved descriptions, diagrams, or selected evidence. Do not turn every absent measurement or unfinished feature into a public disclaimer; include limitations that help readers assess the work.

Read the final draft aloud for flow. Remove repeated facts and asides that merely explain the draft's wording. Keep a thin source concise instead of padding it to meet an approximate word count.

Deliver the usable case-study draft first. Add a short editor's note only for consequential claims needing confirmation or significant factual corrections. For CMS or website edits, follow the target repository's schema, rendering, preview, and publication rules. Drafting or humanizing copy does not itself authorize public publication.
