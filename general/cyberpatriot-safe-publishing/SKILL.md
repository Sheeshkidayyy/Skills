# CyberPatriot Safe Publishing

Prepare a useful public draft and give the user a clear review decision. The goal is defensive learning material that is safe to share, not operational competition support.

## Classify the material

Identify whether the input is a source list, guide, script description, repository page, field note, or media asset. Establish its intended audience and whether it is general learning content or makes season-specific claims.

If the active project has a CyberPatriot publishing policy, read it before reviewing material.

If a screenshot or other visual artifact has unknown provenance, or could be derived from a competition image, scoring report, or restricted material, classify it as **hold** immediately. Do not upload, render, transcribe, inspect, or send it to another tool or AI service. Request an original, non-competition replacement or a safe text-only description instead.

For season-specific rules, policies, or claims, verify against an official current source before presenting them as current. Cite the source in the draft.

## Public-safety review

Before drafting, review each supplied artifact for public-share suitability. Public material must exclude:

- competition images or image artifacts;
- answers, passwords, flags, scores, or team-identifying information;
- restricted prompts, documents, training material, or derived solutions;
- credentials, private infrastructure details, personal data, or non-public media.

Classify the result as **ready**, **revise**, or **hold**. For revise or hold, explain the smallest safe change needed. Do not reproduce unsafe material in the review output.

## Draft the public entry

For ready or revised material, produce a draft containing:

1. a clear title and practical learning goal;
2. a brief, non-sensitive summary of what it covers;
3. intended audience and prerequisite knowledge;
4. source or repository links with attribution where relevant;
5. a scope statement that sets defensive and competition-safe boundaries;
6. a final review checklist and the proposed visibility.

Use the target project's existing content model. Add public references to the project's source list or attribution fields, and mark a public-safety review complete only after it has actually been completed.

## Approval gate

Always stop with a draft and review result. Ask for explicit user approval before changing content to published, uploading it publicly, pushing it, or otherwise releasing it.
